a. Tea in bed.

b. Pick up Stephen Gill’s *The Pillar* from the post office, and look slowly through it in the armchair. Read Karl Ove Knausgård – it’s beautiful. In the second half he writes about Gill telling the story of two pigeons who had their eggs stolen by a goshawk. The next spring, the pigeons laid them in the same place, and the goshawk stole them again, and again the next spring, too. Knausgård writes how the pigeons could do this because “they lack any conception of past or future, living their lives wholly and fully in the now, and in the now there is no goshawk.” He then pivots to Heidegger, who said the animal is ‘poor in the world’, a captive of its surroundings and functions even if they do live fully in the now. I hear both ideas, but my favourite part is the end, where Knausgård says that Gill’s photographs remind him that such ideas “belongs to thoughts, to the writing desk and to books, whereas the birds are in the real world”.
